What this course is

Scientific English looks intimidating from the outside — electrocardiogram, hyperthermia, photovoltaic, cryptography — but it is one of the most regular vocabularies in the language. Almost every term is assembled from a small, reusable stock of Greek and Latin roots, prefixes and suffixes. Learn the pieces and you can read words you have never met before. Over 15 weeks this course works through the terminology of medicine, computer science, physics and the general sciences, always coming back to the same question: what are the parts, and what do they mean?

The five areas we cover

Four subject domains, plus the word-formation strand that runs underneath all of them.

🩺
Medicine
Anatomy · conditions · procedures
Body systems, symptoms and diagnoses — cardio-, -itis, -ectomy, hyper-/hypo- and the rest of the clinical toolkit.
💻
Computer Science
Hardware · software · networks
Where computing borrows from Greek and Latin (crypt-, -ware, cyber-, bin-) and where it invents its own vocabulary.
⚛
Physics
Matter · energy · measurement
Forces, particles and units — thermo-, photo-, electro-, -dynamics — and the SI prefixes from nano- to giga-.
🔬
General Sciences
Biology · chemistry · earth science
The shared vocabulary of the laboratory and the field: classification, process and method terms used across every science.
🧩
Word-Formation
Roots · prefixes · suffixes
The spine of the course. Take a term apart, name each piece, and rebuild the meaning — the skill every other unit depends on.
