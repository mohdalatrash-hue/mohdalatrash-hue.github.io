2Note-Taking Mini-Lesson ~20 min

The fastest way to remember a lecture is to write down the key ideas in your own words right after you hear them — the topic, the main idea, and two or three supporting points.

Lecturers often signal their structure with set phrases. Listen for them: "Today I want to talk about…" (topic) · "The main point is…" (main idea) · "For example…" / "For instance…" (supporting detail) · "To sum up…" / "So, in short…" (conclusion).
Exercise — Listen for the signposts

Read this short passage aloud once. Students close their books and just listen; then read it again while they take notes.

"Today I want to talk about why first impressions matter so much in job interviews. The main point is that interviewers often decide within the first few minutes whether they like a candidate — and that early feeling colors everything that follows. For example, a candidate who smiles, makes eye contact, and greets the interviewer by name is often rated as more competent later in the interview, even if their answers are no better than another candidate's. So, in short, how you begin an interview can matter as much as what you say during it."

  1. What is the topic of the talk?
  2. What is the main idea?
  3. What example does the speaker give?
Show answer key
1. First impressions in job interviews   2. Interviewers decide quickly whether they like a candidate, and this colors the rest of the interview   3. A candidate who smiles, makes eye contact, and uses the interviewer's name is rated as more competent

8Grammar & Pronunciation Drill ~30 min

The auxiliary verbs do, be, and have are used to make questions and negative statements, and in speech they often shrink into contractions.

Simple present/past → use do/does/did: "Does he like her? He doesn't like her." "Did they arrive? They didn't arrive."
Continuous → use be: "Is she reading? She isn't reading." "Was he working? He wasn't working."
Perfect → use have: "Has she left? She hasn't left."
A. Basic — rewrite as a negative statement
  1. I trust him. →
  2. She judged the book by its cover. →
  3. They are forming a good impression. →
Show answer key
1. I don't trust him.   2. She didn't judge the book by its cover.   3. They aren't forming a good impression.
B. Intermediate — rewrite as a question
  1. He has changed his mind about her. →
  2. They were discussing first impressions. →
  3. She assumes people are honest. →
Show answer key
1. Has he changed his mind about her?   2. Were they discussing first impressions?   3. Does she assume people are honest?
C. Pronunciation — say it with a contraction

Practice saying each sentence with the contracted form: she's, they're, doesn't, isn't, we've, didn't, wasn't, hasn't.

  1. She is very reliable. → She's very reliable.
  2. They are not familiar to me. → They aren't familiar to me.
  3. We have already met before. → We've already met before.

9Speaking & Unit Assignment ~40 min

A polite conversation keeps going by taking turns — asking a question back rather than letting the topic drop.

Useful phrases to keep a conversation going: "What do you think?" · "Do you agree?" · "Right?" · "How about you?" · "You know?" · "OK?"
A. Paired discussion (use the phrases above — keep it going for at least 3 turns each)
  • Who was your most memorable teacher, and what impression did they make on you?
  • Have you ever formed a bad first impression of someone that later changed? What happened?
  • Do you think first impressions from social media photos are accurate? Why or why not?
B. Unit Assignment — Have a Discussion

With a partner, discuss the unit question one more time, now using what you've learned today: Are first impressions accurate? Consider: how much does appearance matter versus behavior? When should you trust a snap judgment, and when should you wait and think more carefully? Try to use at least two vocabulary words and one auxiliary-verb question from today's lesson.

Check and reflect
  • I can use the four aspects of first impressions (babyfaceness, familiarity, fitness, emotional resemblance)
  • I can tell the difference between an unconscious and a conscious decision
  • I can use do/be/have to make questions and negatives
  • I can use contractions naturally in speech
  • I kept a conversation going using turn-taking phrases
