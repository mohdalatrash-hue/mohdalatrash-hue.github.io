How stress affects your body

Unit 5 · Health & wellness · Sharon Horesh Bergquist (TED-Ed) · 4:27

Instructor-selected practiceUnit 5 · Health & wellnessNot graded

About this exercise

What long-term stress does to the heart, the stomach, the immune system and more. Medical vocabulary, so listen twice.

Video length4:27
LevelHarder
Note formatRisks → outcomes chart
Time needed40–50 minutes

Unit 5 note-taking skill: separating risks and outcomes using a chart. Unit 5 listening skill: listening for different kinds of numbers. This is extra practice: it uses a real talk and its own vocabulary, so it does not replace the textbook activities or the Teams homework.

Routine: 1 Predict → 2 Watch once (main idea, few notes) → 3 Watch again and take notes → 4 Check with a partner → 5 Answer the questions → 6 Summarise from your notes → 7 Speak.

1Before you watch

Key words

chroniclasting a long time, or happening again and again
hormonea chemical in the body that sends messages
blood pressurethe force of blood pushing on the blood vessels
immune systemthe body’s defence against infection
infectionan illness caused by bacteria or a virus
appetitethe wish to eat
craveto want something very much
insurmountabletoo big or difficult to deal with

These are extension words for this video. They are not from the textbook word list.

Before you watch: stress before an exam. How does your body feel? List three body parts that change.

3Watch again and take notes

Pause or replay as often as you need. Write keywords, not sentences. Useful symbols: → leads to   ↑ more   ↓ less   = means   & and   w/ with   b/c because   # number   e.g. for example.

Risks → outcomes chart

Use one row for each body system. Risk = what stress does. Outcome = the health problem that can follow. Keep it short; use arrows (→) and ↑ ↓.

4Check your understanding

Comprehension questions

Use your notes first. You may watch again. Then press Check my answers: the feedback shows the time in the video where the answer is.

1.True or false? In the short term, stress can be useful.
2.Which hormone makes the heart beat faster and raises blood pressure?
3.What can happen when stress hormones damage the inside of blood vessels?
4.True or false? Cortisol can make you eat less.
5.What is visceral fat?
6.What does chronic stress do to the immune system?

Listen for numbers and counts

Type the number you hear, as a digit.

1.The speaker names stress hormones (cortisol, adrenaline and one more).

2.At the end of the talk, the speaker lists other effects of chronic stress (acne, hair loss, headaches …).

Complete the risks → outcomes chart

Choose the outcome that matches each risk.

1.Adrenaline makes the heart beat faster.

2.Cortisol harms the lining of blood vessels and plaque builds up.

3.Stress signals go from the brain to the nerves of the gut.

4.High cortisol makes you crave comfort foods.

5.Chronic stress weakens some immune cells.

6.Chronic stress is linked to shorter telomeres.
