Listening & Note-taking Lab

Five real talks. One note-taking skill each. Questions you can check as many times as you like.

Instructor-selected practiceNot graded

How this lab works

Each exercise uses a real video by a real speaker, not a recording made for learners. The pages guide you step by step:

  1. Predict and learn eight key words.
  2. Watch once for the main idea. Write only a few keywords.
  3. Watch again and take notes in a frame that matches the unit’s note-taking skill.
  4. Answer questions (main ideas, details, numbers, matching). The feedback tells you where in the video each answer is.
  5. Think further, summarise from your notes only, then speak with a partner.
Rule of the lab: the point is not to understand every word. The point is to catch the main ideas, key details and numbers and to write them quickly.

Five habits of good note-takers

  • Write keywords, not full sentences.
  • Leave space so you can add details after the second listening.
  • Always write numbers with what they measure (for example, “92% of adults”).
  • Listen for signal words: first, however, because, for example, in conclusion.
  • Compare your notes with a partner. Add what you missed in a different colour.

More free listening sources

Use these for extra practice. These sites keep their own copyright, so link to them; do not copy or re-upload their files.

  • TED-Ed and TED Talks: short animated lessons and talks, with English subtitles and transcripts. Choose videos under 6 minutes first.
  • VOA Learning English: news and science stories read slowly, with audio and text on the same page.
  • BBC Learning English: 6 Minute English: short discussions with audio, transcript and vocabulary.
  • ELLLO: free listening lessons with different speakers, accents and quizzes.
  • Randall’s ESL Cyber Listening Lab: listening quizzes at easy, intermediate and difficult levels.
