How stores trick you into buying

Instructor-selected practiceUnit 4 · Business & MarketingNot graded

About this exercise

Shelf height, music, colours and prices: how shops make you spend more. Great for sorting facts from opinions.

Video length8:56
LevelMedium
Note formatMind map + fact / opinion
Time needed40–50 minutes

Unit 4 note-taking skill: using a mind map to note opinions. Unit 4 listening skill: identifying fact and opinion. This is extra practice: it uses a real talk and its own vocabulary, so it does not replace the textbook activities or the Teams homework.

Routine: 1 Predict → 2 Watch once (main idea, few notes) → 3 Watch again and take notes → 4 Check with a partner → 5 Answer the questions → 6 Summarise from your notes → 7 Speak.

1Before you watch

Key words

marginthe profit a shop makes on an item
incentivea reason to do something, often money
subconsciouslywithout knowing you are doing it
strategiccarefully planned to get a result
placementthe position where something is put
layouthow the parts of a place are arranged
aislea path between shelves in a shop
comparative pricingshowing prices side by side so one looks better

These are extension words for this video. They are not from the textbook word list.

Before you watch: think of a supermarket you know. Where do they put sweets and drinks? Why? Write your idea.

2Watch and listen for the main idea

First listening: do not try to write everything. Listen for the main idea and write only a few keywords. Then answer the two questions.

Main idea

Choose the best answer.

1.What is the main purpose of the talk?
2.Which of these is not mentioned as a store trick?

3Watch again and take notes

Pause or replay as often as you need. Write keywords, not sentences. Useful symbols: → leads to   ↑ more   ↓ less   = means   & and   w/ with   b/c because   # number   e.g. for example.

Mind map: how stores make you spend more

Write the ideas under each branch in keywords. Put an F next to a fact and an O next to an opinion.

How stores make us spend more
Write each number and what it measures.
Write it here so you can see what you missed the first time.

4Check your understanding

Comprehension questions

Use your notes first. You may watch again. Then press Check my answers: the feedback shows the time in the video where the answer is.

1.Where are the high-margin products such as candy, gum and soda usually placed?
2.What does “decision fatigue” mean in this talk?
3.Where do stores put the most expensive items with the highest margins?
4.Why are sugary cereals and snacks placed at a child’s eye level?
5.Why are essential items like milk put at the back of the store?
6.True or false? The speaker says warm colours such as red, orange and yellow often attract people into stores.
7.Why do people think $6.99 is cheaper than $7?

Listen for numbers

Type the number you hear (digits only).

1.Americans spend about $ in the supermarket every week.

2.More than million people go to a grocery store in the U.S. every day.

3.If stores get you to spend $10 more, that is million dollars extra profit every day.

Fact, opinion, or claim with no source?

Decide for each statement. Fact = can be checked. Opinion = a personal view. No source = he says it is true but does not name where the information comes from.

1.Candy, gum and soda are placed near the cash registers.

2.“It’s not their fault.” (about shoppers who spend too much)

3.“Research shows that happy people spend more money.”

4.He asked sixth graders, and all of them said they would buy the $6.99 item.

5.“Will you keep falling for them? Probably.”

After checking: Think: his survey used only sixth graders. Does that prove that adults also prefer $6.99? Why or why not?

5Think further
