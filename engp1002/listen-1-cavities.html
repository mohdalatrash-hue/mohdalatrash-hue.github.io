What causes cavities?

Unit 2 · Food · Mel Rosenberg (TED-Ed) · 4:45

Instructor-selected practiceUnit 2 · FoodNot graded

About this exercise

Sugar, bacteria and teeth: a clear chain of causes and effects. Very useful for dentistry students.

Video length4:45
LevelEasier
Note formatCause → effect chain
Time needed40–50 minutes

Unit 2 note-taking skill: taking notes on causes and effects. Unit 2 listening skill: listening for causes and effects. This is extra practice: it uses a real talk and its own vocabulary, so it does not replace the textbook activities or the Teams homework.

Routine: 1 Predict → 2 Watch once (main idea, few notes) → 3 Watch again and take notes → 4 Check with a partner → 5 Answer the questions → 6 Summarise from your notes → 7 Speak.

1Before you watch

Key words

cavitya hole in a tooth caused by decay
bacteriavery small living things; some cause disease
enamelthe hard outer layer of a tooth
dentinthe softer layer of a tooth under the enamel
acida chemical that can destroy materials, including tooth enamel
carbohydratea food substance such as bread, rice or potato that the body turns into sugar
fluoridea mineral added to toothpaste to make teeth stronger
consumeto eat or drink

These are extension words for this video. They are not from the textbook word list.

Before you watch: what do you think makes holes in teeth? Write three ideas.

4Check your understanding

Comprehension questions

Use your notes first. You may watch again. Then press Check my answers: the feedback shows the time in the video where the answer is.

1.True or false? The bacteria that cause cavities usually come from our mothers’ mouths when we are babies.
2.What do the bacteria make when they consume sugar?

3.The bacteria are resistant to acid, but our are not.

4.True or false? A cavity hurts as soon as acid starts to wear down the enamel.
5.Why did ancient people get cavities even though they ate little sugar?
6.Why did cavities become much more common after the Industrial Revolution?
7.What does fluoride do?

Listen for numbers

Type the number you hear. (Write digits, e.g. 25.)

1.The ancient human remains were thousand years old.

2.About thousand years ago, humans were already using sharpened flint to remove bits of rotten teeth.

3. percent of American adults have had cavities.

Put the cause–effect chain in order

Choose 1 to 5 (1 = first).

1.Bacteria use the sugar and make acid.

2.The tooth may need to be taken out or filled.

3.Someone eats food with a lot of sugar.

4.Acid takes calcium from the enamel and opens a path into the dentin.

5.Damage goes past the dentin, the nerves are exposed and the tooth hurts.
