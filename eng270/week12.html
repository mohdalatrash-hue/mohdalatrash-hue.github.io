Core ideas

What administrative texts are

Administrative texts are associated with the governing bodies of any entity. They include documents connected with the legal issues of such bodies — for example the regulations of a company, or the responsibilities attached to a job (job descriptions). They are therefore usually linked with legal texts, and the language features of the two are very similar.

This is why the course places administrative texts immediately before legal texts: Weeks 12–15 form a single register block.

The register

Most of the vocabulary in these texts consists of terms and idioms usually associated with courts and public authorities. These texts are undoubtedly very formal, without much use of literary features.

They also differ from language to language, so the translator is advised to become acquainted with the terminology of both the source and the target texts. That is a research task, and it is the task this week sets.

Why they are, in fact, translatable

For the most part administrative texts have a relatively strict language style that can easily be translated. That is not a contradiction of their formality — it is a consequence of it. Where a genre restricts style, it restricts choice, and restricted choice is what makes a translation decision tractable.

The genre is close to legal language because it deals with formal sets of rules or regulations to be followed and administered within the entity concerned. Compare this with Week 7: literary translation offers you thirty defensible renderings; an administrative text offers you one or two.

Check yourself
Why are administrative and legal texts treated as one register block?
Because administrative texts are associated with the governing bodies of an entity and include documents connected with their legal issues, so they are usually linked with legal texts, and their language features are very similar — formal, formulaic, and drawn from the vocabulary of courts and public authorities.
A student says formal texts must be the hardest to translate. Answer them.
Formality restricts style, and restricted style restricts choice. Administrative texts have a relatively strict language style that can, for the most part, be translated straightforwardly. The difficulty is terminological and precisional, not interpretive — unlike literary translation, where the range of defensible renderings is wide.
What preparation does the textbook explicitly advise before translating in this genre?
Becoming acquainted with the terminology of the texts in both the source and the target language, since administrative language differs from language to language.
