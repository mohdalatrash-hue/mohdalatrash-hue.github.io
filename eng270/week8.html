Reading check
In one sentence, why does this lesson argue scientific texts are the most challenging genre on the course?
They combine a total absence of ready-made terms with the need for absolute precision — there is no room for the interpretive flexibility that literary or media translation allows.
Distinguish a calque from a loanword.
A calque is translated, piece by piece, into the target language (its structure is copied but its words are rendered); a loanword is simply retained, largely as-is, borrowed directly from the source language.
Name the three coinage strategies Arabic uses for missing scientific terms.
Derivation, al-istinbāt, and loan translation.
What two levels of analysis does Lesson 20 say must happen before translating a scientific term?
First the lexical level (terminology and register), then the grammatical level (how English structures the sentence around that term).
How does English typically lean grammatically that Arabic does not?
English leans on connectives, the passive voice, and the verbs ‘to be’/‘to have’; Arabic tends to repeat keywords rather than use connectives, and prefers the active voice.
Why should the lexical analysis come before the grammatical analysis, rather than the other way round?
You first need to establish what the term actually means and at what register, before you can decide how to restructure the sentence grammatically around it — grammar serves the term’s meaning, not the reverse.

Core ideas

Why scientific texts are the hard genre

The term science covers physics, biology, chemistry, astronomy, mathematics, geology, biochemistry, astrophysics, ecology and the engineering sciences. Scientific texts differ from other texts basically because scientific language draws on humanistic education (Pinchuk 1977), and because they are set out in unqualifiedly objective terms for the reader of the source and target text alike (Bassnett-McGuire 1991b).

The textbook states plainly that they are more challenging than, for example, literary texts. That is worth pausing on: literary texts are harder to translate well, but scientific texts are harder to translate at all, because a translator who does not understand the content cannot begin.

The difficulty sits in the terminology — a large number of very specific terms and concepts, plus numbers, symbols, graphs and diagrams — and terminology changes continuously, so a five-year-old glossary is a liability rather than an asset.

Lesson 19 · Borrowing, in three forms

Borrowing introduces the source term into the target language, and it is done three ways:

  • Total borrowing — full phonological borrowing, usually called Arabisation. It accepts derivation, so the borrowed stem then behaves like an Arabic noun. Best for new terms, inventions, diseases and discoveries with no TL equivalent.
  • Loan translation, or calque — the new term is translated into the TL, element by element.
  • Naturalisation — the source term is adapted to TL morphology. Common in computing, chemistry and biology.

One definition to keep straight, because it is easy to lose under exam pressure: a calque is a word or phrase taken from one language and translated literally, word for word, into another; a loanword is a foreign word or expression maintained in its original form in the target language.

Lesson 19 · Coinage, in three forms

With coinage, new terms are created in the target language. Baker (1987) gives three basic strategies for Arabic:

  • Derivation — new terms by analogy through Arabic’s own derivation system, nominal and verbal forms from the trilateral root. Described as the most natural way of enriching the language without altering its identity.
  • Al-istinbāt (discovery) — reviving old words by extending their meanings to express new concepts.
  • Loan translation — the meaning of the term rendered literally into Arabic.

Lesson 20 · Analyse before you translate

Accuracy is the major issue: the translation must convey the same objectives as the source. The first step is understanding the scientific content, and that means analysing the text on two levels.

Lexical analysis — look up the technical terms, use a good technical dictionary, and identify the text’s register.Grammatical analysis — a contrastive analysis of the structures of both languages. English is characterised by connectives, little repetition of keywords, extensive use of passive forms, and the verbs to be and to have. Arabic uses more connectives, repeats keywords more often, and favours active structures. Tense and aspect are problematic in both, so the Week 6 rules apply here too.

Where a term has no target equivalent, the common strategy is to leave it in the original language and add a footnote that fully explains it. Scientific translation is a communicative service, offering new information to a new audience so that it can be used easily, properly and effectively (Byrne 2006) — which settles many small decisions: the question is always whether the reader can use it.

Check yourself
Is كمبيوتر a calque or a loanword? And العدد الذري?
كمبيوتر is a loanword — the foreign word kept in its original form. العدد الذري is a calque — the phrase translated word for word.
Which coinage strategy is called the most natural way of enriching Arabic, and why?
Derivation. It builds new nominal and verbal forms by analogy from the trilateral root, so the term is generated by Arabic’s own machinery rather than imported — and the language’s identity is unaltered.
A disease named last year has no Arabic equivalent. Rank your options.
Total borrowing with a footnote is the safest first move, since the term must stay recognisable to specialists. Loan translation works if the name is descriptive. Derivation or al-istinbāt risk producing a term no reader recognises — appropriate only once an authority has adopted it.
Why can a translator with excellent Arabic still fail at a physics paper?
Because proficiency in the target language is not sufficient. Translators need the language of the discipline and its background in order to articulate the ideas competently, following the content and style of the source — which is why the textbook says the scientific translator must be a good researcher.
