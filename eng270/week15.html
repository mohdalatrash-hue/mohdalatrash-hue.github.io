Reading check
What does Lesson 32 say contract translation requires beyond grammar?
Equal command of both languages’ technical legal and commercial terminology, not just grammatical competence — a grammatically correct contract translation can still be legally wrong if the terminology is off.
Why does Lesson 33 insist that current political knowledge is not optional for translating UN or peace-agreement texts?
A balanced, accurate translation of such texts depends on understanding the parties involved and the history behind the agreement — without that context, a translator risks misrepresenting a term or a clause’s intent.
What is the purpose of the Module 7 review, positioned at the very end of the course?
To consolidate across the whole course — recognising which of the five genres a text belongs to, choosing and justifying a translation method for it, and diagnosing difficulties by family (grammatical, lexical, stylistic, phonological) under exam-like time pressure.
According to this final review, what should guide your choice of translation method for an unfamiliar text?
The genre and purpose of the text — mapping it onto what you have learned about literary, scientific, media, administrative, and legal translation — rather than defaulting to a single method regardless of text type.

Consolidation — the shape of the whole course

One axis, five genres

Everything in this course hangs on the axis you met in Week 2. Literal at one end — word-for-word, one-to-one, direct translation. Free at the other — bound free, loose free. Faithful, communicative and adaptive translation sit along it, and the five practical genres each occupy a different stretch of it.

  • Literary (Weeks 5–7) — the widest range; adaptation and domestication are live options, and culture is the main problem.
  • Scientific (Weeks 8–9) — precision plus term creation; borrowing and coinage are the named tools.
  • Media (Weeks 10–11) — plain, fast, front-loaded; the ideological cases are the hard ones.
  • Administrative (Weeks 12–13) — formal and formulaic; consistency is a requirement.
  • Legal (Weeks 14–15) — strictest of all; function determines everything.

The values half of the specification

Two of the course outcomes are not about skill at all. You are expected to follow professional translation ethics, values and standards, including translation quality assurance, and to work effectively and constructively in teams.

Concretely, in this course that means: your translations are your own words; you do not submit work produced by a translation program or application; the use of AI in assignments and projects is prohibited; and you do not copy another student’s translation. Quality assurance means checking your own work before it is checked for you — the unit-by-unit revision habit from Week 3.

How to revise

Work backwards from the vocabulary. For each week, be able to name the concepts and give one example: three literal methods, two free methods, three foundation methods, four problem families, four Graedler steps, four Harvey strategies, domestication and foreignisation, three kinds of borrowing, three kinds of coinage, four categories of media text, three functions of legal texts.

Then practise translating, timed, with a dictionary and nothing else. The practical half of the final exam rewards fluency in Arabic and accurate reading of English, and neither improves by reading about translation.

Lessons 32–33 · Agreements, contracts and the Security Council

Lesson 32 · Agreements and contracts

The process is fraught with challenges, some of them arising from the nature of the material itself. Translating agreements and contracts requires specific knowledge of the subject area, especially because the register has a very formal style and tone — knowledge built through the accumulation of vocabulary and glossaries for the genre.

The translator needs not only a broad knowledge of the source text but a sound knowledge of the target text, and particularly of the equivalent terminologies in it. A good grounding in both languages preserves the meaning of the source and produces an accurate, reliable translation; a grounding in one produces fluent nonsense.

Lesson 33 · Security Council terms and peace agreements

The immediate challenge here is a sound knowledge of current political affairs and of the historical factors governing these agreements. A good knowledge of the conflict and of the parties involved is crucial to a balanced and representative translation — the Week 11 problem, in its most consequential form.

Familiarity with key agreement terminologies is vital, as is familiarity with United Nations formalities and register, which are highly conventionalised and largely fixed in Arabic. A bilingual glossary of key terms and phrases is again the practical answer to the complexity of the vocabulary.

Note in passing that official Arabic texts exist for the major instruments. Consult them after you have produced your own version, never before — the point of the exercise is your judgement, not your ability to find the answer.

Check yourself — across the whole course
Which method would you choose for a road sign, a poem, a company regulation and a newspaper headline?
Road sign — communicative translation: the function crosses, not the wording. Poem — the freest end, possibly adaptation, with phonological features weighed against meaning. Company regulation — faithful and precise, with a fixed terminology list. Headline — reconstruct the headline function in Arabic; do not expand the ellipsis.
Name the four families of translation problem and give one example of each.
Grammatical — English SVO against Arabic VSO. Lexical — polysemy, as in spring. Stylistic — fronting, as in Suicide he committed. Phonological — sound patterning in poetry, where prosody may outrank propositional meaning.
A source text says Thousands were killed in the raid. Give two Arabic renderings and say what separates them.
قتل آلاف في الغارة and استشهد آلاف في الغارة. The first is neutral; the second domesticates by importing a value the English does not carry. In conflict reporting that is a substantive decision, not a stylistic one.
What does ‘translation quality assurance’ mean for a student, in practice?
Checking your own work before anyone else does: revising unit by unit against the source, verifying terminology consistency, confirming that obligations remained obligations and numbers remained numbers, and confirming that the words are yours — not a program’s, not an AI’s, and not another student’s.
