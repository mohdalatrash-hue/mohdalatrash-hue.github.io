Core ideas

Methods versus procedures — a distinction worth one exam mark and a lot of clarity

Before discussing types of translation, one distinction must be made. Translation methods (or strategies) relate to the whole text. Translation procedures (or techniques) are used for sentences and the smaller units of language (Newmark 1988a: 81).

You choose a method once, for the text; you choose procedures constantly, unit by unit. Students who confuse the two end up defending a whole-text decision with a word-level reason.

1 · Faithful translation

Maintains a balance between the literal meaning of the SL word and the TL syntactic structures. It takes context into consideration and aims at producing a more precise meaning of the source text — a reasonable default for most serious prose.

2 · Communicative translation

Renders the exact contextual meaning of the original in such a way that both content and language are readily acceptable and comprehensible to the reader. It aspires to create on the TL reader the same effect the ST created on its own reader — and it communicates the meaning at the expense of accuracy.

It involves some level of cultural approximation, and it is the method of choice for conventional formulae, proverbs and clichés, where the translator replaces a source concept with one that already exists in the target language. It concentrates on the message and the main force of the text, tends to be simple, clear and brief, and is always written in a natural style (Newmark 1988: 48).

Road signs, greetings and compliments are best rendered this way.

3 · Adaptation

The freest form of translation, not usually distinguished from free translation. Used mainly for plays (especially comedies) and poetry: themes, characters and plots are preserved, the source culture is converted to the target culture, and the text is rewritten. You will meet it again in Week 7, where the drama material treats it in detail.

The four families of translation problem

A translation problem is any difficulty that stops you translating — forcing you to check, recheck or rewrite before you can make sense of it. They fall into four families, and naming the family is usually most of the solution.

  • Grammatical — complicated SL grammar; different TL grammar (English sentences are always verbal, Arabic sentences are verbal or nominal); different word order (English SVO, Arabic verb-first in verbal sentences).
  • Lexical — synonymy, polysemy and monosemy, collocations, idioms, proverbs, metaphors, Arabisation of technical terms, proper names and institutional titles, and culture.
  • Stylistic — formality versus informality, fronting, parallelism, ambiguity, complex versus simple style, sentence length, active versus passive, repetition and variation, redundancy, nominalisation versus verbalisation, and the translation of punctuation.
  • Phonological — sound and its effect on meaning; the beauty of language, and decisive in poetry.

Two warnings the textbook is firm about. Punctuation marks are meaningful and carry a stylistic function. And it is not true that the English passive is normally turned into the Arabic active — both should generally be kept.

Check yourself
Communicative translation is said to sacrifice accuracy. Accuracy of what, exactly?
Accuracy of wording and of literal semantic content. What it protects instead is effect — the response of the target reader. For a road sign, a greeting or a proverb, effect is the meaning that matters, so the trade is correct. For a contract or a lab report it is not.
Give an example of polysemy and one of monosemy.
Polysemy: spring, which can be نابض / نبع / ربيع / يقفز. Monosemy: speak يتكلم. Polysemy is a lexical problem precisely because the dictionary offers you four doors and only context tells you which one.
Sort these into families: (a) Suicide he committed; (b) to walk on water; (c) an English sentence with no main verb equivalent in Arabic.
(a) Stylistic — fronting, done on purpose for emphasis, and ignoring it damages meaning. (b) Lexical — an idiom, whose meaning cannot be read off its words; يصنع المعجزات. (c) Grammatical — different TL grammar, since Arabic sentences may be nominal.
Is a method the same as a procedure? Answer in one sentence.
No: a method relates to the whole text, while a procedure is used for sentences and smaller units.
