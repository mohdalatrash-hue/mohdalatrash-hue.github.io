Week 2 workshop · Translation methods · the literal family

Three Kinds of Literal

Word-for-word, one-to-one, and literal translation of meaning — told apart on real sentences, and each pushed until it breaks.

🎯 What this workshop trains

Students lose marks by using the phrase ‘literal translation’ for three different methods. This workshop makes you name which one you are using, and predict where each one fails before it fails.

📖 Reading → Ghazala (2012), methods of translation; Lahlali & Abu Hatab, Lessons 1–2 (pp. 2–8). Note that the two books define literal translation slightly differently — the questions below cover both.
🔍 Diagnose the version. Each pair below shows an English sentence and an Arabic attempt. Name the method — and, where it fails, name the failure.
✍️ Translate each sentence twice — once word-for-word, then by literal translation of meaning — and keep both. Seeing them side by side is the point of the exercise.
📝 Week 2 review — 10 questions on the literal family, its risks, and the difference between the two textbooks’ use of the term.
