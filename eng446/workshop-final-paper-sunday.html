Warm-up: Abstract X-ray
8 minutes · analysis only; do not write your abstract yet

Find

Open the abstracts of Papers 01, 07, and 09. In each, label five moves: problem/context → purpose → method → main result → implication.

Imitation test: If one sentence performs two moves, mark both. If a move is missing, note what uncertainty it creates for the reader.

Whole-Paper Anatomy Relay
12 minutes · see the paper as one argument

Relay

  1. The PDF navigator opens your best-matched paper.
  2. The move hunter writes every major heading in order.
  3. The skeptic asks what question each section answers.
  4. The scribe draws arrows showing what each section passes to the next.
1
Introduction: Why is this study needed?
2
Literature review: What is already known, disputed, or missing?
3
Method: How was each RQ answered?
4
Results: What evidence was produced?
5
Discussion: What does that evidence mean?
6
Conclusion: What can responsibly be taken forward?
2
Title Surgery
15 minutes · specificity without a mini-abstract

Compare

Use Papers 04, 06, and 09. Underline the intervention/tool, outcome, population/context, and—only if useful—design. Notice that brand names are included only when central to the study.

Published does not mean perfect. A title can be accurate yet too long, too causal for its design, or too vague about participants. Imitate precision; edit excess.
Avoid “impact/effect” if your evidence is only correlational or perceptual.0 words

3
Introduction Funnel: Five Moves
22 minutes · broad problem to unavoidable RQ

Mentor hunt

Read the final two pages of the introduction in Papers 06 and 09. Locate where the authors stop reviewing the field and start constructing the present study.

1
Problem: establish the educational/language-learning problem in the real context.
2
What is known: summarize the strongest relevant evidence, not the whole field.
3
Tension: show inconsistency, limitation, missing population, or missing outcome.
4
Gap + significance: say exactly what remains unknown and why Qassim/this population matters.
5
Purpose + RQs: state what this study does without announcing results.
Pressure test: Delete the gap sentence. If the study still feels necessary, the earlier paragraphs already built the gap. If the study no longer feels necessary, strengthen the evidence leading into the gap.
Avoid dictionary-style openings, unsupported claims that AI is “revolutionizing everything,” paragraph-by-paragraph article summaries, a gap based only on “few studies,” and a purpose that introduces variables never reviewed.
4
Research-Question Alignment Matrix
15 minutes · every noun creates a methodological obligation

Use Papers 05 and 09

Trace each RQ into participants, instrument, analysis, result, and discussion. Then perform the same trace on your group paper.

Red flags: “impact” with no comparison or baseline; “perceptions” with no perception instrument; “improvement” with no before/after measure; two outcomes but only one is analyzed.
5
Literature Review: Synthesis, Tension, Landing
21 minutes · stop stacking summaries

Three-paper sentence map

Use the literature review in Papers 01, 04, or 07. Find one paragraph containing at least three sources. Label each sentence: claim, supporting pattern, contrast/qualification, interpretation, or landing.

  1. Select three sources already in your reference list that address the same idea.
  2. Write a topic sentence that makes a claim, not an announcement.
  3. Combine at least two sources in one comparative sentence.
  4. Add a genuine contrast, boundary, or methodological difference.
  5. Land the paragraph on what your study therefore needs to examine.
Aim for evidence + relationship, not one source per sentence.0 words
