Guided Reading: How a Published Paper Holds Together
17 minutes · Paper 01, then compare one matched paper

Answer from the PDF

  1. What exact problem and gap justify the study?
  2. Where does the introduction become the present study?
  3. Which noun in each RQ becomes a measured variable?
  4. Why is the design quasi-experimental rather than fully experimental?
  5. What must a reader know about participants, grouping, intervention, and instruments to reproduce it?
  6. How are validity and reliability addressed?
  7. In what order are descriptive and inferential results reported?
  8. Where does the discussion first move from “what happened” to “what it means”?
  9. How does the paper connect a finding to previous research without claiming identical evidence?
  10. Which implication is supported directly, and which recommendation needs more caution?
Comparison challenge: Find one place where your topic-matched paper does the move better than Paper 01 and one place where it does it worse. “Published” is the start of analysis, not the end.

Methodology Mirror: Planned versus Actually Done
18 minutes · write the study you conducted

Use Papers 01, 04, or 09. Locate these submoves: design, setting/participants, sampling, instruments, procedure, ethics, and analysis. Then compare your proposal’s plan with what your group actually did.

Integrity rule: The final methodology reports the completed study, not an idealized plan. Report changes transparently; never invent participants, reliability values, procedures, or statistical tests.

Results/Data Analysis: RQ-to-Evidence Ladder
17 minutes · report before you explain

Compare Papers 01, 02, and 09. Identify how each result subsection signals the RQ, names the analysis, points to a table/figure, reports the key value, and states the result without interpreting causes.

1
Orient the reader to the RQ or outcome.
2
Name the analysis and any assumption/reliability evidence that matters.
3
Direct the reader to a numbered table/figure; do not repeat every cell.
4
Report the few values that answer the RQ: n, M/SD or frequencies, test statistic, p, and effect size when available.
5
State the result in plain language with the correct level of certainty.
Table-to-prose test: Hide the paragraph—can the table still be understood? Hide the table—does the paragraph still tell the main answer? If both duplicate every number, revise.
4
Discussion Builder: The Seven-Move Paragraph
30 minutes · evidence becomes a responsible claim

Study the first main discussion finding in Papers 01, 06, and 07. The exact order varies, but strong discussions perform these moves.

1
Answer: state the finding in relation to the RQ—without copying the results table.
2
Evidence: remind the reader of the most relevant evidence.
3
Connection: compare with specific earlier research.
4
Agreement/contrast: name whether the relationship supports, extends, complicates, or contradicts prior work.
5
Explanation: offer a plausible mechanism grounded in design, context, or theory.
6
Implication: explain why the finding matters for teaching, learners, or research.
7
Qualification: bound the claim by sample, instrument, duration, context, or alternative explanation.
Use verbs such as supports, extends, contrasts with, or may reflect.0 words
