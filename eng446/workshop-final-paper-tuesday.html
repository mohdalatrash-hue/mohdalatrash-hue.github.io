Warm-up: Reverse Abstract
7 minutes · reconstruct the paper from its ending

Open the conclusion of Paper 06 or 09. Without rereading the abstract, write five bullets predicting the paper’s problem, purpose, method, main result, and implication. Now compare with its abstract.

Conclusion: Answer, Synthesize, Bound
15 minutes · no new evidence, no literature-review replay

Compare the conclusions in Papers 01, 05, 07, and 09. Identify four moves:

1
Return: restate the purpose/problem briefly.
2
Answer: synthesize the main answer to each RQ, not every statistic.
3
Contribution: state what the study adds in this context.
4
Boundary/forward look: end with a warranted implication or research direction.
Do not introduce a source, variable, or result for the first time.0 words
2
Recommendation Traceability Lab
12 minutes · every recommendation must earn its place

Use Papers 02, 07, and 09. For each recommendation, trace backward: recommendation ← implication ← finding ← evidence ← RQ. If a link breaks, weaken, revise, or remove it.

Specific beats grand: prefer “instructors could pilot…” over “universities must adopt…”. A small, short, local study rarely justifies system-wide policy.
3
Abstract Written Last: Five-Move Compression
18 minutes · whole paper, zero decoration

Return to the abstracts of Papers 01, 06, and 09. Build one or two sentences for each move: context/problem, purpose/RQs, method, main results, conclusion/implication.

No citations unless explicitly required; no result that does not appear in the paper.0 words

Reference List: Two-Way Audit
15 minutes · citations and references must reconcile

Do not only proofread the reference list. Reconcile the two systems in both directions:

Pass A: text → list

Highlight every in-text author–year citation. Confirm one matching reference-list entry with the same authors and year.

Pass B: list → text

Mark every reference-list entry. Confirm it is cited meaningfully in the text; remove uncited leftovers unless required as background reading.

5
Whole-Paper Coherence Locks
20 minutes · seven cross-section inspections

Assign one lock to each member. Inspect across the entire paper, then report one break and one repair.

1
Terminology lock: tool, outcomes, population, and group labels stay identical.
2
RQ lock: every RQ has method, evidence, discussion, and conclusion.
3
Number lock: sample sizes, duration, item counts, table values, and percentages agree.
4
Tense lock: completed procedures/results use past tense; established knowledge uses present where appropriate.
5
Claim lock: causal language never exceeds the design.
6
Citation lock: borrowed claims are cited and reference entries reconcile.
7
Navigation lock: headings, table numbers, cross-references, and order are correct.
6
Editor Check
