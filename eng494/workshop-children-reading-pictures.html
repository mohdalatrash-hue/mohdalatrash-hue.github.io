Reading Pictures

📖 The reading behind this workshop

Lathey (2016, Ch. 3, “Translating the visual”) cites Hoole’s 1659 translation of Comenius’s Orbis Pictus, where the translator already worries about words that no longer fit the pictures. Today we start from Hoole’s own plates.

1

Look first: the Master and the Boy

5 min
Look questions · 5 minutes · cover the text, read only the picture
  1. Who is bigger, and who is holding something? What does each posture tell you about their relationship?
  2. What is the setting (weather, buildings, distance)? What could an Arabic reader bring to this landscape?
  3. Which gesture would you describe in words, and which would you leave for the picture to show?
  4. If the Arabic text said Come, boy, I will teach you in a very formal register, would it suit these two figures?
Notes after you have written something
The picture is already a speech situation: an older man with a staff and a child with a hat in hand. The register of the Arabic invitation should match this relationship (respectful but warm, not lecturing). This is the first lesson of Lathey’s chapter: the picture constrains what the words can do.
2

Compare: numbers, labels, jars

8 min
Inscription audit · 8 minutes
  1. Numbers. On the Orbis Pictus plate each creature carries a number that points to a word in the text. If your Arabic list of names puts the words in a different order, what breaks? Who has to fix it, you or the printer?
  2. The label. The jar in Lear’s boat carries an English label. The text of the poem tells us the jar holds honey. Choose: keep the label, gloss it in the text, or ask the printer to re-letter it (عسل). Write one sentence defending your choice.
  3. Colour. The poem says pea-green boat; the picture is black and white. What does the Arabic text have to do (and not do)?
Notes
Numbers: numerals are inscriptions that bind words to a picture. If the Arabic order differs, the numbers must be re-keyed or the order kept; this is a production decision, not a translator’s private choice (Lathey, 2016). Label: if the artwork cannot be changed (co-production), the text carries the meaning or the label stays; if it can, re-lettering in Arabic is cleanest. Colour: the picture cannot show it, so the words must (لونُ البازلاء الأخضر). Do not explain what the picture already shows.
