What this project is

This is a take-home group project worth 10 marks. Each group of two students produces a full translation of two poems — one adult poem and one children's poem, song or limerick — both chosen by your group on the sign-up sheet at the bottom of this page.

The project has two parts of equal weight. Each part follows the same three-piece structure: a first draft, a second draft, and a written annotation. Every external source must be cited in APA 7th edition, in-text and in a reference list.

⚠️ Academic integrity — read this before you start.
Using AI-generated text in any part of this assignment — the translation drafts or the annotation — will result in a ZERO. This includes machine-translation output (Google Translate, DeepL and similar) presented as your own work. The translation and the reflection must be written by the two of you. Databases and search tools may be used to find your scholarly source; they may not be used to write it up.
The two parts
🖋️
Part 1
Adult Poetry Translation
5 marks
The task

Translate the entire adult poem your group selected on the sign-up sheet, from English into Arabic, using one of the following course strategies:

Free Verse Translation
Abandon the source metre and rhyme; carry the imagery, tone and line-logic in unrhymed Arabic. You must still account for what the form was doing.
Rhymed Translation
Reproduce a rhyme scheme in Arabic. It need not be the source scheme, but it must be a real scheme, held consistently, and you must be able to name it.

Strategy reference: Lahlali & Abu Hatab, the lesson on poetic translation covered in Week 4, and the Poetry workshop (six strategies) and Rhyme & Phonemic workshop on this site.

What you submit for Part 1
  1. 1
    First Draft
    Your initial full translation of the entire poem. Do not polish it away — the marker wants to see where you started.
  2. 2
    Second Draft
    A revised and improved version, refining your chosen strategy (the free-verse line or the rhyme scheme). The difference between the two drafts is itself evidence.
  3. 3
    Annotation — minimum 250 words
    A written reflection containing all three of the elements below.
Inside the Part 1 annotation
① Strategy discussion
Explain which strategy you chose and why, and discuss the common challenges translators face with adult poetry. Name the specific problems your poem gave you, not problems in general.
② Course source quote — Landers
Include a direct quote from Landers, "Some Notes on Translating Poetry," and explain its relevance to your process. Cite in APA. In-text: (Landers, 2001, p. 97)
Reference: Landers, C. E. (2001). Literary translation: A practical guide. Multilingual Matters.
Use the page number of the passage you quote in your own copy.
③ External scholarly quote
Include a direct quote from one additional scholarly source on the challenges of adult poetry translation, with elaboration and an APA citation. Suggested databases: Google Scholar, SDL (Saudi Digital Library), Scispace, Elicit, Research Rabbit, RefSeek.
🎶
Part 2
Children's Poetry, Song or Limerick
5 marks
The task

Translate the entire children's poem, song or limerick your group selected on the sign-up sheet, using one of the same two strategies:

Free Verse Translation
No rhyme — but for a child text you must still answer for rhythm, sound-play and read-aloud quality.
Rhymed Translation
Keep a scheme. For a limerick that means holding AABBA; for a song it means lines that still fit the tune.

Strategy reference: apply the concepts from "Translating Sounds and Reading Aloud" (Lathey, 2016, Ch. 5) covered in Week 5, and the Children's Poetry & Song workshop — singability, the sing-or-clap test, animal cries, wordplay and nonsense.

🔊 The test that matters here

A children's translation is not finished on the page. Read your second draft aloud — or sing it — before you submit. If it cannot be said or sung at speed by a child, it is not yet a children's translation, however accurate it is.

What you submit for Part 2
  1. 1
    First Draft
    Your initial full translation of the entire poem, song or limerick.
  2. 2
    Second Draft
    A refined version after revision and after reading it aloud.
  3. 3
    Annotation — minimum 250 words
    A written reflection containing all three of the elements below.
Inside the Part 2 annotation
① Issues discussion
Reflect on the specific challenges you met translating children's poetry — rhythm, sound and onomatopoeia, cultural adaptation, singability, the fixed tune, the picture the words sit beside. Say which of them beat you.
② Course source quote — "Translating Sounds and Reading Aloud"
Include a direct quote from the Week 5 reading and explain how it shaped your translation decisions. Cite in APA. In-text: (Lathey, 2016, p. 89)
Reference: Lathey, G. (2016). Translating children's literature. Routledge.
The reading is Chapter 5, "Translating sound: Reading aloud, poetry, wordplay and onomatopoeia." Cite the chapter's own page number as it appears in your copy.
③ External scholarly quote
Include a direct quote from one additional scholarly source on children's poetry translation, with elaboration and an APA citation. Same suggested databases as Part 1.
APA 7th edition — how to cite

All sources must be cited both in-text inside the annotation and in a References list at the end of your submission. Follow APA 7th edition.

The two course sources you already have
Landers, C. E. (2001). Literary translation: A practical guide. Multilingual Matters. Lathey, G. (2016). Translating children's literature. Routledge.
Direct quotes

A direct quote needs quotation marks, the author, the year and the page number: Landers (2001) argues that "…" (p. 97). A quote without a page number does not earn the mark. A quote dropped in without elaboration does not earn the mark either — say what it does for your translation.

Journal article, for your external sources
Author, A. A. (Year). Title of the article. Title of the Journal, volume(issue), pages. https://doi.org/xxxx

A fuller guide is in the APA quick reference on the ENG 446 site.
