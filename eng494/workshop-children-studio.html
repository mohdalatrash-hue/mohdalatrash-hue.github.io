Compare: which version serves the child?

6 min
Two Arabic versions (written for this exercise)

The English is the first stanza of “The Owl and the Pussy-cat” (Lear, 1871).

Version A (close to the words):
ذهبَ البومُ والقِطّةُ إلى البحرِ في قاربٍ أخضرَ جميلٍ، أخذا بعضَ العسلِ ومالًا كثيرًا، ملفوفًا في ورقةِ نقدٍ.
Version B (re-voiced for the ear):
أبحرَ البومُ والقِطّةُ معًا، في زورقٍ أخضرَ كالبازِلاءِ، حملا العسلَ وكثيرَ النقودِ، ملفوفةً في ورقةٍ زرقاءِ.

Which one would you rather read aloud to a five-year-old?

Rubric

  • Voice: does it sound like a story told to a child?
  • Sound: does it have a beat or rhyme when read aloud?
  • Picture fit: does it agree with the plate and avoid explaining it?
  • Faithfulness: does it keep what the English says (honey, money, note)?

What to notice

Version A keeps the facts but is flat when read aloud. Version B gains rhythm and a vivid colour comparison (pea-green becomes كالبازلاء) but adds a colour for the note (زرقاء) that Lear does not give: it invents a detail. A strong version would keep B’s rhythm and drop that invented colour. Translators always trade; the rubric makes the trade visible.
