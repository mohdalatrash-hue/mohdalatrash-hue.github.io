Hear it: Hoole’s vocal alphabet

Woodcuts from the “vocal alphabet” of Orbis Pictus (Hoole, 1659; 1887 reprint). In Hoole’s English the creatures crieth, blaiteth, chirpeth and saith. Public domain; Project Gutenberg eBook #28299.
Cry-match · type your Arabic cry first, then reveal

For each creature write the Arabic verb and the cry a child would say aloud. Then compare.

Reveal: one set of options
الغُرابُ ينعِقُ: «غاق غاق» · الحَمَلُ يَثْغو: «ماع ماع» · الجُندُبُ يَصِرّ: «صِرْ صِرْ» · الهُدهُدُ: «هُدْ هُدْ»
Illustrative sound-words; check them against Arabic children’s books you know. Note that the English hoopoe and the Arabic هدهد are both often explained as imitating the bird’s call: the name is the sound.

Letter it: how does a sound look?

6 min
Lettering lab · ding dong

In a picture book the shape of a sound-word is part of the picture (Lathey, 2016, drawing on Zanettin). Arabic has no italics or capitals, but it has other tools. Here are three treatments of the same sound:

دِنْج دُونْج1 · plain, same size
دِيييييـنْج   دُوووووونْج2 · stretched letters (tatweel / long vowels)
دِنْج دُونْج3 · small then large, like a bell swinging

Discuss

There is no single right answer. Ask: which would a five-year-old read aloud correctly? Which suits the artwork, if the bell is drawn swinging? Remember that Zitawi found Arabic publishers favouring forms that a child still learning Arabic can read (see Lathey, 2016, p. 64), so legibility is part of the decision.
