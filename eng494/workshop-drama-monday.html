01

Guided Reading Jigsaw

30 min · expert groups → mixed groups

Open each evidence cluster. Your group answers one cluster, records a location or example from Landers, then teaches it to a mixed group.

A · Speakability and the “first time”

1. What does speakability mean? 2. Why can meaning, precision, fidelity and even style become secondary? 3. What breaks the “illusion of the first time”? 4. What did Sunday’s Actor Trips activity demonstrate?

B · Bentley’s four “phenomena”

5. Define meanly literal, faithful translation, adaptation and variation. 6. Which can honestly be called translation? 7. Why must the third and fourth advertise their nature?

C · Text, performance and collaborators

8. In what dual sense does a play exist? 9. Who are the translator’s unseen collaborators? 10. What cultural or extratextual information may actors and directors need? 11. What belongs in dialogue, gesture, staging or a note?

D · Thought-by-thought, puns and effect

12. Why can word-by-word fidelity sound wrong? 13. What is thought-by-thought translation? 14. Why do puns and self-referential language force adaptation? 15. What may be sacrificed to preserve comic effect?

E · Farce, modernization and limits

16. Why are farces often adapted? 17. Why introduce topical or local references? 18. What dominant effect must survive? 19. Can only part of a work be adapted? 20. Why does adaptation demand experience and restraint?

02

Bentley’s Courtroom

15 min · claim → evidence → cross-examination

Classify each disputed production, then prepare a two-sentence defence using Landers. The opposing team may challenge your label.

Case 1

Correct, cogent Arabic stays close to the source but adjusts word order so actors can deliver it.

Speakability does not automatically turn a faithful theatrical translation into adaptation.

Case 2

The setting moves to Riyadh; scenes are cut; names, jokes and institutional relationships change.

The work may retain its structure and effect, but accumulated liberties require the adaptation label.

Case 3

Only the premise and two character functions remain; a new plot, ending and dramatic argument are written.

This approaches a new play “based on” the source: Bentley’s variation.

Case 4

Every word is represented, but the Arabic is stiff, unidiomatic and nearly impossible to perform.

Formal closeness alone does not produce the correct and cogent version Bentley calls translation.
03

Modernization Control Room

15 min · change one variable at a time
Production problem
A comic scene depends on a servant delivering a sealed letter late, while two characters mistakenly believe it contains an invitation from a powerful patron.

Choose which elements to modernize. For every selected change, state the dramatic function preserved and the risk introduced.

04

المكتب Adaptation Autopsy

14 min · invariant → variable → audience effect

An official format can travel without every line travelling. Analyse what the production must preserve to remain recognizably The Office, and what should change for a Saudi workplace.

Do not judge localization only by similarity of words. Ask whether the embarrassment, hierarchy, comic timing and audience complicity still function.
05

Wordplay Emergency Room

12 min · save wording, meaning or effect?
Teacher-written comic emergency
DIRECTOR: You said the figures were sound.
ACCOUNTANT: They were. Then the auditor heard them.

The English joke moves between “sound” meaning reliable and something heard. Produce three treatments.
